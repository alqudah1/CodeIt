# CodeIt promotion briefing

## The message

### One sentence

CodeIt helps students turn an idea into a real website, game, or quiz, then
learn, edit, save, and share the code behind what they built.

### Thirty-second explanation

Most beginner coding products start with lessons and an empty editor. CodeIt
starts with something the student wants to create. Its AI-assisted studio makes
a working first version, then the student explores the code, completes guided
lessons and exercises, improves the project, and can publish it. Parents can
confirm progress emails so they see real milestones such as completed lessons
and published websites.

### The problem CodeIt solves

- Blank code editors make the first step intimidating.
- Tutorial-only products can feel disconnected from what a student wants to
  create.
- General AI website builders can make something quickly but do not necessarily
  teach the student how it works.
- Parents often pay for learning products without seeing what was learned or
  created.

### The promise

Start with an idea. Leave with something real.

Build it. Learn how it works. Make it yours. Share what you created.

## Who we promote to

### Primary buyer

Parents and guardians of beginner students aged 13–17 who want creative,
project-based coding and understandable progress.

### Primary user

Students with an idea for a game, quiz, personal page, fan page, portfolio, or
school project.

### Partnership audience

Teachers, tutors, libraries, coding clubs, camps, and after-school programs.

Do not promote direct account creation to children under 13 until CodeIt's
parent-managed signup flow is complete. Content may still be useful to parents
of younger learners, but the adult must be the call to action.

## What the promoter should demonstrate

Use one continuous two-minute story:

1. Say: “A student starts with an idea, not a blank editor.”
2. Ask CodeIt to build a simple project, such as a space quiz.
3. Show the working result immediately.
4. Change one visible detail.
5. Open the code and show the beginner-friendly explanation.
6. Show a related lesson or exercise.
7. Save and publish the project.
8. Show the example parent milestone email.

The proof is the workflow. Avoid long feature lists before showing it.

## Calls to action

Use only one call to action in each piece of content.

- Student-facing organic content: **Build a free project.**
- Parent-facing content: **Join the Founding Family waitlist.**
- Educator outreach: **Book a short product walkthrough.**

The Founding Family plan is currently a proposed US$12/month pilot. No billing
is live, so never say that somebody can purchase it today.

## Content that does not require a presenter on camera

### Series 1: “I asked CodeIt to build…”

Record the screen from prompt to working result. Use a seven-word hook and large
captions.

- “I asked it to build a space quiz.”
- “Can a beginner make this reaction game?”
- “A school project became a real website.”
- “One idea. Three different website designs.”

### Series 2: “Change one thing”

Start with the finished project and make one change to prove that the student
has control.

- Change the colours.
- Add a scoring rule.
- Turn a button into an animation.
- Explain one line of code.

### Series 3: “What the student learned”

Connect a visible project behaviour to one coding concept.

- “This score works because of a variable.”
- “This button works because of an event.”
- “This game repeats because of a loop.”
- “This quiz reacts because of an if statement.”

### Series 4: Parent proof

- Show the parent progress email.
- Show a project before and after the student edited it.
- Show “lesson completed → project improved.”
- Explain what a parent can see without reading source code.

### Reusable format

- 9:16 vertical screen recording.
- 15–35 seconds for most posts.
- Show the result in the first two seconds.
- Add captions; sound must not be required to understand the video.
- End with one action and the CodeIt URL.
- Reuse the original recording for YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, and TikTok.

## First 30 days

### Week 1: Establish the story

- Publish three short project demonstrations.
- Publish one parent-progress demonstration.
- Publish one simple “what CodeIt is” carousel.
- Contact five parents for a 15-minute product reaction.

### Week 2: Test hooks

- Repeat the best project format with three different hooks.
- Create one tutorial page and video around a real CodeIt lesson.
- Contact five teachers, tutors, libraries, or coding clubs.
- Record where viewers stop watching and which link they click.

### Week 3: Build trust

- Share one real learner project with permission.
- Publish a founder explanation of why CodeIt teaches after building.
- Turn common parent questions into three short posts.
- Invite qualified parents to the Founding Family waitlist.

### Week 4: Choose what to scale

- Keep the two content formats producing the most qualified site visits.
- Stop formats that receive views but no project starts or parent interest.
- Interview at least five people who started but did not save a project.
- Do not begin paid advertising until the organic message produces repeatable
  project starts or waitlist signups.

## Weekly operating rhythm

- 3 short project or lesson videos.
- 1 parent-trust post.
- 1 useful search article or lesson page.
- 10 direct partnership messages.
- 5 short user or parent conversations.
- 1 review of conversion data.

Quality matters more than posting every day. YouTube states that there is no
minimum Shorts cadence required for a video to perform.

## What to measure

Views alone are not success.

### Awareness

- Qualified visits to the homepage
- Percentage of video viewers who reach the call to action

### Student activation

- Builder opened
- First project generated
- Project saved
- Project published
- A second session within seven days

### Parent demand

- Pricing page viewed
- Founding Family interest recorded
- Parent interview booked
- Progress email confirmed

### Partnership demand

- Educator reply
- Demo booked
- Pilot group started

Initial learning targets—not promises:

- 100 qualified visits per week
- 25 first projects
- 10 parent waitlist signups or interviews
- 3 educator demonstrations

## Paid promotion rule

Start with adults, not children. If paid promotion begins:

- Target parents, guardians, educators, tutors, and coding program operators who
  are 18+.
- Use the parent benefit and student project proof in the same advertisement.
- Begin with a small controlled test only after organic messaging converts.
- Do not use uploaded child-user lists, personalized under-18 targeting, pressure
  children to ask parents to buy, or imply guaranteed academic outcomes.

TikTok, Meta, and Google restrict advertising personalization and targeting for
people under 18. TikTok also prohibits pressuring teens to persuade parents to
purchase. Review current platform policy before every paid campaign.

## Search promotion

Build useful pages around real tasks students and parents search for:

- coding for kids and teens;
- beginner Python projects;
- how to make a quiz in Python;
- website project ideas for students;
- AI website builder that teaches coding;
- coding progress for parents;
- beginner coding games.

Each page should contain a real explanation, an interactive example, and a
relevant next step. Do not generate dozens of thin pages. Google recommends
people-first content and says structured data should accurately represent the
visible page.

## Access the promoter should receive

- A clean demo student account with no real child data
- The approved logo, colours, Pixel mascot, and font list
- A folder of screen recordings and project examples
- Approved one-sentence and thirty-second descriptions
- Campaign links with consistent UTM labels
- Read-only access to a simple weekly performance dashboard
- A list of claims that are approved and claims that must not be made

Do not give the promoter AWS, database, production admin, or real student access.
