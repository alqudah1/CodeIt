# CodeIt investor meeting guide

## The 45-second opening

CodeIt is a creative coding studio for young people ages 5–18. Most coding
products ask beginners to learn syntax before they make anything exciting.
General AI builders make things quickly, but they often hide the learning.
CodeIt connects both sides: a student begins with an idea for a website, game,
or quiz, gets a working first version, then learns by changing, understanding,
saving, and sharing the code behind it.

The product works today. We are pre-revenue and are now validating the family
value proposition, activation, retention, and willingness to pay. We are
looking for experienced partners who can help us turn a working product into a
trusted and repeatable education business.

## The four-minute demonstration

1. Open the investor overview and explain the problem in one sentence.
2. Open the project studio.
3. Use: “Build a colourful space quiz with three questions, a score counter,
   and confetti when I finish.”
4. Show the working result before explaining the interface.
5. Change one visible detail, such as a colour, label, or rule.
6. Show the code connected to that change.
7. Explain that a student can save and publish the project.
8. Return to the investor overview and show the current stage and next proof
   points.

Do not spend the meeting clicking through every lesson, game, or dashboard.
The core story is one idea becoming one understandable project.

## The honest business explanation

- Primary user: young creators ages 5–18.
- Primary buyer: parents and guardians.
- Ages 5–12: parent-guided public exploration until the verified
  parent-managed account flow is complete.
- Free experience: lets a family try the core creation-and-learning loop.
- Planned family plan: US$12 per month. Billing is not live yet.
- Later expansion: tutors, clubs, camps, libraries, and schools after the
  family value proposition is proven.

## Questions investors will ask

### How many customers do you have?

We do not have paying customers yet. The product is working, but we are at the
validation stage. The next milestone is a small Founding Family pilot that
measures activation, retention, parent interest, and willingness to pay.

### What is different from ChatGPT or an AI website builder?

Those products optimize for producing an answer or finished website. CodeIt is
designed around a learning loop: build, change, understand, save, and share.
The goal is not only that a project works; it is that the student gains control
over it and can explain what they changed.

### What is different from coding-course products?

Many courses begin with lessons and syntax. CodeIt begins with the learner's
idea and uses the resulting project as the reason to learn the code.

### What proof do you have?

We can demonstrate the product and measure the core funnel. We do not yet claim
proven product-market fit. The evidence we are now seeking is qualified traffic,
completed first projects, meaningful personalization, saves, return use, parent
interest, and paid conversion.

### How will you acquire customers?

The first tests combine short project-reveal videos, parent-value content,
creator partnerships, direct parent interviews, and partnerships with tutors,
clubs, camps, and libraries. Every channel uses its own privacy-safe link so we
can compare qualified visits and project starts rather than optimizing for
views alone.

### What will funding accelerate?

Customer discovery, product and onboarding refinement, verified parent-managed
accounts, parent-approved progress infrastructure, curriculum quality, safety
and privacy work, and repeatable distribution.

### How much are you raising?

Do not invent a number in the meeting. Say:

> We are defining the smallest milestone-based round that can fund customer
> validation, parent infrastructure, and a repeatable acquisition test. I want
> the amount and milestones to be grounded in a clear operating plan, and I
> would value your perspective.

## What to ask the investor

1. Which part of the product or business thesis feels strongest?
2. What evidence would you need to see before considering an investment?
3. Which risk should we test first?
4. Do you know parents, educators, tutors, or coding-program operators who
   would join a discovery conversation?
5. Who else should see the product?

## What not to claim

- Do not say CodeIt has customers, revenue, or product-market fit yet.
- Do not present US$12 per month as live pricing.
- Do not present parent progress emails as live delivery.
- Do not promise grades, learning outcomes, or career outcomes.
- Do not describe an unverified parent email as parental consent.
- Do not promote independent account creation to children under 13.
